01 · About

Connecting molecular mechanisms to atmospheric chemistry.

I am a postdoctoral research fellow at the University of Washington working with Prof. Joel A. Thornton. My research centers on atmospheric radical multiphase chemistry, with particular emphasis on reactive oxygen species, organic peroxy radicals, hydroperoxyl(HO2) uptake, and aerosol-mediated oxidation.

I combine laboratory kinetics, chemical ionization mass spectrometry, UPLC-MS, field observations, and observation-constrained modeling to understand how microscopic reaction pathways alter HOx cycling, ozone sensitivity, peroxide budgets, and secondary aerosol chemistry.

02 · Research

Radical chemistry across gas, particle, and aqueous phases.

My work links reaction mechanisms with measurements and models across laboratory and atmospheric scales.

HO2

HOx and peroxide budgets

Constraining HO2 uptake by tropospheric aerosols and aerosol-phase peroxide formation to determine their effects on radical cycling, ozone production, and volatile-organic-compound sensitivity.

  • Flow tube
  • LIF
  • PKU-MARK
  • Multiphase modeling
RO2

Organic peroxy radical chemistry

Resolving radical pathways in OH-initiated oxidation, including hydrogen abstraction in isoprene chemistry and RO2 self-reaction branching measured through chemical ionization mass spectrometry.

  • CIMS
  • Radical-omics
  • Isoprene
  • Mechanisms
ROS

Aerosol oxidation and reactive oxygen species

Investigating metal-catalyzed aerosol chemistry, sulfate formation, and gaseous and particulate reactive oxygen species through integrated instrumentation, field observations, and chemical box models.

  • UPLC-MS
  • Orbitrap
  • Field studies
  • ROS
